Visual language

Colour here is not decoration. Colour is a claim about evidence.

Archaeological drawing has distinguished the extant from the reconstructed for centuries: standing masonry is drawn solid, conjecture is hatched. This site adopts that convention as its entire visual system — in line with the London Charter (2009) and the Seville Principles (2011). No colour on these pages, in the map or in the model means “antiquity”; each one states how firmly a claim stands.

Standards

Paradata, not decoration

Every element is tied to its archaeological source and a confidence level: A fact, B analogy, C hypothesis. The target stack for phase P2 is HBIM with CIDOC-CRM / CRMarchaeo alignment, FAIR paradata and a WebGPU presentation layer.

Method and standards (SR)→

What this is not — yet
  • No new field data: this is a synthesis of published literature and public reports.
  • No formal agreement with the custodian institutions; this is preparation for that conversation, not its outcome.
  • No HBIM yet — a schematic working model that functions as a spatial knowledge registry. The pilot with real survey plans is phase P2.
  • Neither the model layout nor the map overlay is georeferenced.
  • The bibliography is incomplete; every hypothesis is marked UNVERIFIED until tested.
Collaboration

A technology partner, not a competitor

Finds, rights and publication remain with the custodian institutions and their researchers. What we bring is infrastructure: field documentation in 3D space, standards-compliant paradata, and a citable publication.

Repository and issue tracker→

A tool for managing field documentation, tracking conservation state in 3D space, and publishing to the London Charter standard — raising the citation count and visibility of the researchers involved.
